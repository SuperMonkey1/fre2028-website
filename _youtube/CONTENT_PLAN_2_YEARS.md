## How to read the estimates (be skeptical of them)

- **Not data.** I don't have your channel analytics. These are judgment calls based on how niche climbing and maker channels typically behave. After video 3, compare the real numbers to column "Likely" and multiply every later estimate by the same ratio.
- **"Views" = long-form views in the first 90 days.** Evergreen videos (search topics) keep collecting afterwards; spectacle videos mostly don't.
- **Three numbers per video:** *Low* (it flops), *Likely* (the middle outcome), *High* (it catches a wave: Reddit, Hacker News, press, a big collaborator share). High is rare. Expect 3–4 of 34 videos to land there.
- **What moves the number**, in order of importance: (1) channel size at publication, (2) whether a non-climber can understand the title, (3) how visual and "weird" the thing is, (4) search demand for the topic, (5) an outside audience that shares it (Jakob, press, forums).
- **Channel stage at publication:** S1 under 1k subscribers, S2 1–5k, S3 5–15k, S4 15k+. The same video is worth 2–5× more at S3 than at S1, which is why the biggest builds sit later in the plan.

## Shorts and Instagram

Each long video produces 3 Shorts. Expect **300–3,000 views each**, occasionally 30k+. Plan one Short per week even in weeks without a long video. Their job is to feed the newsletter and the shop, not to be the main source of views.

## What this means for your goals

1. **The "Likely" channel is healthy but modest.** About 245k long-form views in 90-day windows, plus long-tail and Shorts, gives roughly **350–500k total views** by LA, and about **8–15k subscribers**. The 25–50k in the strategy doc needs two or three videos from the "High" column, so treat 25–50k as the stretch case, not the plan.
2. **YouTube alone will not sell "a lot".** If about 40% of views come from videos that feature a product, and you get 1–3 orders per 1,000 of those views, that is roughly **200–400 orders in two years**, around **€10–20k**. A bigger result needs more products (the ideas list has several), the newsletter, gyms and Instagram. Treat the channel as the engine for trust and the list.
3. **Do the cheap videos.** Tier C videos reach about the same audience as Tier B for a fifth of the work. During heavy training blocks, publish those.
4. **Order matters little; format matters a lot.** Titles a non-climber understands (#6, #10, #12, #24) have the highest ceilings. Titles only a climber understands (#18, #20) are floors. Put at least one high-ceiling video in every quarter so the channel keeps getting a chance to break out.
5. **Recalibrate at video 10.** If your real numbers are two times lower than "Likely", lean toward Tier C and series. If they are two times higher, move the high-ceiling builds (#10, #12, #24) earlier.
