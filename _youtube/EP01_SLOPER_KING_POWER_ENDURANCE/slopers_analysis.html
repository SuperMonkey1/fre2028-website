    What we can learn for the Sloper King video

    • Wrist strength is the open gap, and it is our thesis. The top comment under Send Edition’s sloper Short (358 votes) says wrist curls made them better at slopers. Someone else calls it the first time they have seen wrist strength mentioned; another comment (79 votes) says no creator covers the strength side of slopers. Our “4 muscles instead of 2” claim is what viewers are asking for. Lead with it.
    • People already want tools, and they build their own. Under Lattice’s Heavy Roller video, viewers made their own wrist wrench, asked for a workout in an app and asked where to buy weights. This supports a tool plus a protocol or app, not a tool alone. One comment (18 votes) warns about re-injuring the TFCC with too much weight, so the video needs a clear “start light” safety segment.
    • The audience argues about method. The most-liked comment on the Beastmaker sloper video (370 votes) says sloper hangboards are for open-hand strength, and that if a half-crimp works better then your half-crimp is stronger. Viewers will challenge any test that does not say exactly what it measures. Define the measurement before showing results.
    • Physics-literate viewers check claims. There are several threads on “friction does not depend on surface area”, with replies that skin deforms and fills the hold’s texture. For the “added mass makes slopers easier” fact, test it on camera, use the correct terms (normal force, contact pressure) and show data, or the comments will do it for us.
    • Failing in public wins trust. A comment with 863 votes on Hannah Morris’s video thanks her for showing the failing and the progress instead of only sends, and another (979 votes) praises encouraging coaching. Your 2025 World Championships failure is an asset: show the fails with the tool too, and keep the tone encouraging.
    • Visual explanations get the best reactions. The most-liked request on Emil Abrahamsson’s “7 Lessons” (274 votes) asks for more “nerdy stuff” and praises the visualisation of force on the hands. Under Lattice’s video, viewers praise the replay shown next to the explanation. Use overlays for the muscle chain and a split-screen replay.
    • Skin and humidity are a recurring practical problem. There are comments about humidity at the World Cup final (139 votes), skin durability and a skin product, and contradictory advice on sweaty versus chalked hands. A short segment or a Short on skin and chalk with the device would be useful, and 220-grit contact should be checked for comfort.
    • Injury talk is mixed. Some viewers like slopers because they feel less painful than crimps; others report wrist tendon pain on slopers. Do not claim “pulley-friendly” as fact. Present it as a hypothesis and cite the literature review in this folder.
    • The references viewers already use are Beastmaker and Lattice. Position Sloper King against them in one sentence (portable, wrist-focused, cable or pull-up bar), not as another hangboard.
