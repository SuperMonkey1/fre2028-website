# Blueprints: The Open-Drone "Engineering Detective" Storytelling Blueprint

> **The Core Philosophy:** Never let the viewer feel stupid. Blend high-stakes real-world adventure with visually brilliant, math-free explanations. Make them feel like an insider to a secret engineering trick.

---

### Step 1: The "Show, Don't Tell" Hook (0:00 - 0:15)
*Never start at the desk with a lecture. Start at the extreme physical limit of what the engineering can do.*

- **The Modern 2026 YouTube Rules:**
  - **Zero Opening Rituals:** NO channel logos, NO animated bumpers, NO "Welcome back to the lab / my channel", and NO recap. Cold traffic didn't "come back"—they clicked a specific thumbnail and want instant gratification.
  - **Immediate Physical Object / Action (Within 3 Seconds):** Put the weird 3D-printed tool in hand or show the dramatic failure on the wall immediately.
  - **Natural Prosthetic Visibility (Non-Negotiable):** Fré's prosthetic foot/leg must be visible naturally from frame 1 (at the bench, on the mat, or on a hold). Never treat it as a mid-video plot twist. Visible competence instantly earns deep viewer respect without words.
  - **The Iceberg Narrative (No "Road to LA" Shouting):** Never open with "Welcome to my Paralympic prep". Cold traffic clicked to see a crazy build and a high-stakes test. Let the LA 2028 quest be discovered organically through workshop details, whiteboard countdowns, and elite performance.
  - **Lower-Third Credential Anchor (@ 0:15):** Flash a crisp 2-second on-screen lower-third graphic:  
    `Dr. Fré Leys // PhD Mechanical Engineering // 2x World Cup Gold`  
    Never spend valuable spoken audio listing your degrees; let the screen do the talking while you explain the bold hypothesis.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:** Standing 4km away from a transmitter with a dense forest in the way, proving the signal is still perfect.
- **Fré's Example:** *"This 3D print will either hold 200 kg or shatter on my fingers."* (Clanking weights on screen).
- **Creator Prompt:** What is the most extreme, high-tension end-result of your experiment? Put the camera on that exact moment in the first 10 seconds.

---

### Step 2: The Anchor to Everyday Frustration (0:30 - 1:00)
*Connect the niche technology to a universal, everyday experience.*

- **The Tactic:** Contrast the incredible capability of your topic with an everyday frustration the viewer understands.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:** *"How can this go so far, but your Wi-Fi connection at the same frequency dies at the end of your garden?"*
- **Creator Prompt:** What everyday tech/concept does your audience struggle with that makes your topic look like a superpower by comparison?

---

### Step 3: The "Open Loop" Mystery (1:00 - 2:00)
*Gamify the video to lock in audience retention until the final minute.*

- **The Tactic:** Introduce a puzzle, a "broken rule," or an Easter egg early in the video. Tell the audience it's there, but don't explain it until the end.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:** Laying out four receivers and stating: *"One even violates a general design rule, and I bet you can't tell which one it is."*
- **Creator Prompt:** Find a counter-intuitive detail, a weird design choice, or a hidden flaw in your subject. Point it out early, challenge the viewer to figure it out, and promise the answer at the end.

---

### Step 4: The "Ping-Pong" Pacing (Throughout)
*Prevent boredom by alternating between "University Lecture" and "Vlog Adventure."*

- **The Tactic:** Do not sit at a desk for more than 2-3 minutes at a time. Use a real-world "mission" as the backbone of the video, and cut to the studio/desk only when you need to explain the theory behind what is happening outside.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:** Cutting back and forth between biking further and further away to test the range, and sitting at the desk explaining the radio frequencies making it possible.
- **Creator Prompt:** What is the physical, real-world "test" you can do for this video? Treat the test as your A-Story, and the technical explanation as your B-Story.

---

### Step 5: The "Kindergarten Physics" Method (The Educational Core)
*Ban complex math. Use brilliant analogies and visual graphs.*

- **The Tactic:** Take highly abstract, mathematically heavy concepts and translate them into physical, real-world objects or sounds.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:**
  - *Concept:* 2.4GHz wavelength penetration. *Analogy:* Comparing it to visual light blocking vs. low-frequency sound.
  - *Concept:* Thermal noise floor. *Analogy:* "The background hiss of the universe."
  - *Concept:* LoRa Modulation. *Analogy:* A literal bird chirp collapsing into a single bin.
- **Creator Prompt:** If you have to explain a formula or a dense process, how would you draw it using only everyday objects, animals, or basic nature elements?

---

### Step 6: The "Myth-Buster" Climax (The Shareability Engine)
*Give the viewer a "smart fact" to share with their friends.*

- **The Tactic:** Pay off the "Open Loop" mystery (from Step 3) by debunking a common misconception. When you teach a viewer something that "even the professionals get wrong," they will share the video to look smart to their peers.
- **The 'Open Drone' Example:** Revealing the "rule-breaking" RF via on the circuit board, and then aggressively debunking the myth of how ground currents work: *"Current flows in circles... atom for atom, the ground return current flows underneath the trace."*
- **Creator Prompt:** What is a common myth or misunderstood concept in your field? Use the climax of your video to bust this myth definitively.

---

> **To the Creator:** If you follow this blueprint, you aren't just making a "tech review" or an "explainer video." You are making an **engineering detective story**. Build the mystery, show the evidence in the real world, simplify the clues, and give them a mind-blowing revelation at the end.

## 3. Lean Shooting Playbook (Xiaomi Phone + DJI Osmo Pocket)

You don't need a film crew. Follow this simple system:

### A. A-Roll (Talking to Camera in the Workshop / Lab)
- **Camera:** Xiaomi Smartphone on a mini-tripod at eye level (4K 24fps).
- **Framing:** Rule of thirds (Fré slightly to the left/right, Lab Board visible in background).
- **Lighting:** Position desk lamp or soft window light facing your face (never sit with a bright window behind you).
- **Audio:** Clip the wireless lavalier mic to your shirt collar (pointing toward your mouth, ~15cm below chin).

### B. B-Roll (Visual Proof & Inserts)
*Rule: Never talk about an object or code for more than 5 seconds without showing it on screen.*
1. **The Over-The-Shoulder CAD Shot:** Phone on tripod looking over your shoulder at Fusion360.
2. **The 3D Printer Timelapse:** Phone shooting close-up of nozzle laying down hot filament.
3. **The Macro "Hand Feel" Shot:** Slow, smooth pan across the textured grip surface using the Osmo Pocket.
4. **The Gym Action Shots (Osmo Pocket):**
   - *Shot 1 (Wide):* Full route from floor level.
   - *Shot 2 (Close-up Tension):* Tight shot on fingers slipping vs. gripping.
   - *Shot 3 (Athlete Face):* Eye focus and breathing before pulling onto the wall.

## 5. Production Operations, Seeding Strategy & Community Multiplier

### A. The "3-Video Bank" & Operational Reality
We bank 2 to 3 finished videos before Day 0, NOT because of an algorithmic trick, but for **operational consistency and sanity**:
1. **Consistency Without Panic:** You maintain high engineering standards without rushing or scrambling.
2. **Time to Analyze Retention Data:** You can calmly inspect YouTube Studio's second-by-second retention curves and intro drop-offs before shooting the next project.
3. **Time to Iterate Packaging:** You can A/B test titles and thumbnails without production pressure.
4. **Immediate Binge Depth:** A viewer who discovers Video 1 immediately has Video 2 and Video 3 to watch.
5. **Launch Cadence:**
   - **Day 0:** Launch Video 1 (HW-04: *I Built a Prosthetic Foot That Grips Better Than a Climbing Shoe*).
   - **Day 14:** Launch Video 2 (HW-08: *I Built a Transparent Climbing Wall in My Living Room*).
   - **Day 28:** Launch Video 3 (HW-07: *Can a 3D Print Hold 200 kg?*).
   - **Transition to Sustainable Rhythm:** From Video 4 onwards, settle into the 3-tier production engine.

### B. The 3-Tier Production Engine (Managing Athlete Time)
Never attempt to make every single video a 50-hour blockbuster:
* **Tier A (Flagship Films — 20–50+ hrs):** Bionic foot, Transparent wall, Schubert collab, Extreme prosthetics (4–6/yr).
* **Tier B (Medium Builds — 10–25 hrs):** Sloper King, Uneven hangboard, Force sensors, Kilter analytics (4–6/yr).
* **Tier C (Low-Production Insights — 2–8 hrs):** Failure analysis, quick gear tests, competition debriefs, raw lab experiments.

### C. Algorithm Ignition: External Seeding Playbook (Videos 1–6)
For videos 1–6, external distribution must do the initial heavy lifting:
- **r/3Dprinting & r/BambuLab (Tier A Maker):** Share slicer profiles, CAD cross-sections, and slow-motion fracture GIFs.
- **r/climbharder (Tier B Biomechanics):** Technical writeup on wrist torque, tendon creep, or individual finger length ratios.
- **Hacker News ("Show HN"):** Genuinely HN-shaped for Video 2 (*"Show HN: I thermoformed a transparent modular climbing wall in my living room"*).
- **Belgian Press & IFSC Network:** Sporza, VRT, and IFSC athlete community for the Bionic Foot story.
- **Jakob Schubert Collab (Video 5):** Agree on the promotional package (joint Instagram Collab Reel + YouTube community tab shoutout) *before* shooting.

### D. The Thumbnail Testing System (One Clear Idea, No Dogmatic Face Rule)
There is no universal "faces lift CTR by 30–50%" rule. Sometimes a human face is great; sometimes the hardware alone is 10x more arresting.
1. **One Instantly Recognizable Idea:** Zero visual clutter, instant readability on mobile screens.
2. **Hypothesis Testing (A/B Test):** Test (A) Fré's focused face holding the hardware vs. (B) The raw hardware in action (e.g. *carbon bionic foot gripping a 2mm rock crystal on the wall*).
3. **Signature Palette:** Dark industrial workshop slate (`#18181B`) + Electric Blue / Cyan (`#00E5FF`) or High-Vis Neon Yellow (`#E2F952`) rim lighting and accents.
4. **Typography:** Maximum 2–4 words, heavy industrial sans-serif, positioned top-left or top-right (strictly avoiding the bottom-right timestamp badge).

### E. Multi-Channel Waterfall (Per Video)
1. **Companion Short 1 (Day 0):** High-tension vertical cut launched simultaneously with the long video.
2. **Companion Short 2 (Day +3):** 30-second biomechanical hack or 1000 fps slow-mo fracture.
3. **Companion Short 3 (Day +7):** Sensor telemetry readout or failed print lesson.
4. **LinkedIn Post (Dutch):** Thought leadership post on biomechanics problem-solving, tagging local tech partners.
5. **Newsletter (`fre2028.la`):** Deep dive email to the community sharing the behind-the-scenes build.

## 6. The "Shoot Once, Extract 3 Shorts" Engine & Pre-Flight Retention Checklist

### A. The "Shoot Once, Extract 3 Shorts" Workflow
Never shoot Shorts separately. During every single long-form shoot, extract **3 distinct vertical Shorts / Reels (9:16, 20–50 seconds)** directly from the project footage:

1. **Short 1: The Break / Fail Moment (High-Tension Curiosity Magnet)**
   * *Visual:* 1000 FPS slow-motion fracture of a 3D print under 160 kg, or slipping violently off a sloper.
   * *Hook:* *"Everyone said PLA would hold. Here's what happens when you load 160 kilos onto it."*
   * *Purpose:* Viral algorithm test; drives cold traffic to the full build.
2. **Short 2: The 30-Second Biomechanical Hack (Actionable "Weetje")**
   * *Visual:* Quick direct-to-camera test with hand/fingers (*e.g., "Look at your hand right now: your fingers aren't the same length"*).
   * *Purpose:* Instant viewer value and comment-section debate.
3. **Short 3: The Live Sensor / Data Spike (Tech Geek Factor)**
   * *Visual:* Hold on the wall with live Bluetooth load cell telemetry spiking to 850N during a dynamic move.
   * *Hook:* *"How many Newtons does a dynamic latch actually put on your tendons?"*
   * *Purpose:* Proves high-tech authority and engineering credibility.
