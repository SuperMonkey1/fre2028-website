# The 20-Video Series: "How It Works (so I built my own)"
> 💡 **CONTEXT & SERIE-CONCEPT:**  
> Dit document definieert het overkoepelende YouTube-format ("How X Works, so I built my own").  
> De actuele productietijdlijn en maandelijkse targets staan vastgelegd in **[CONTENT_PLANNING_16_MONTHS.md](file:///c:/Users/frede/Documents/GitHub/fre2028-website/_youtube/CONTENT_PLANNING_16_MONTHS.md)** en **[DOELSTELLINGEN_CHART_16_MAANDEN.md](file:///c:/Users/frede/Documents/GitHub/fre2028-website/_youtube/DOELSTELLINGEN_CHART_16_MAANDEN.md)**.

Why this format: the OpenDrone channel (24.5k subscribers) got 99k, 230k, 363k and 834k views on its last four videos using "How X works (so I built my own)", after years of small videos. Their pattern, which I'm copying:
- A teaching spine where each concept leads to a design decision, so the explanation *is* the build.
- An open loop in the first minute that is paid off late.
- One counterintuitive fact per video.
- The built thing is tested for real at about 70% of the runtime.
- Each video points to the previous one, so the channel reads as one series.
- Trust first, selling later, and the sale itself explained as a mission.
- **One domain.** Every OpenDrone video is about drone hardware, so a viewer who stays for one wants the next. That is why this series has **no off-topic videos** (a hummingbird video was removed for exactly this reason).

**The domain:** gear for the human hand and body. Grip, holds, climbing physics, prosthetics, and the data behind them. **Channel promise:** *I explain how climbing and the body work by building my own gear. I'm a PhD engineer and a Paralympic hopeful, so the stories are real.* Climbing and LA 2028 are the setting and the long story, and the maker angle (3D printing, casting, sensors, CAD) is how each topic gets built and tested.

## Estimate method
- **Views = long-form views in the first 90 days.** Low / Likely / High. "Likely" assumes the format works partly, not like OpenDrone's best videos. Reaching their level needs a breakout.
- **Orders** only count sales that come from the video itself, using 0.2–0.8 orders per 1,000 views when a product is just mentioned and 2–2.5 per 1,000 when the product *is* the topic. These rates are assumptions to replace after the first launch.
- **Calibrate early:** if videos 1–3 average under 2,000 views, divide all later estimates by 3. If they average over 15,000, multiply the later ones by 2 and bring the bionic foot forward.

## The 20 videos

### Phase 1: Prove the format (Nov 2026 – Jan 2027)

**1. Nov 2026: How your fingers hold on (so I built a crimp for mine)**
*Pitch:* Look at your hand: your fingers are all different lengths, yet every hangboard is flat. I'll show how tendons and pulleys share the load, what goes wrong with a flat edge, and how the FingerPrint generator makes an edge that fits *your* hand. Open loop: a quick measurement that predicts which finger is overloaded.
*Why first:* the tool already exists, so it can ship by mid-November with no new build. It sits in the core domain (hands and grip), the viewer can do something (measure their hand), and it feeds the newsletter with a free file. The pitch is the explanation, not the product.
*Views:* 1,000 / **5,000** / 50,000. Curiosity hook anyone understands, shareable tool, but a zero-subscriber start. *Orders:* ~10.

**2. Dec 2026: How 3D prints break (so I broke 50 climbing edges)**
*Pitch:* Everyone says 3D-printed gear is dangerous. I'll print the same climbing edge in PLA, PETG and carbon nylon, in different orientations, and pull each to failure in a test rig at 1,000 fps. Open loop: which part holds the most, and it's not the one you'd guess.
*Why here:* it needs only your workshop, so it's ready in time. It has the widest reach of the early videos (makers, 3D-printing forums), and it earns the trust needed to sell load-bearing products later.
*Views:* 2,500 / **15,000** / 150,000. Destruction tests travel on Reddit and Hacker News. *Orders:* ~8.

**3. Jan 2027: I built a custom grip for World Champion Jakob Schubert**
*Pitch:* I take a cast of Jakob's hand, scan it, model it and make a training grip shaped exactly for him. Then he tests it. Beyond the star power, it shows why "one size fits all" holds break down at the top, and how a one-off becomes something anyone can get.
*Why here:* a ready audience for the month when people start training again, and the natural step after #1 (a custom edge for one person, then for everyone). If Jakob's schedule slips, move it back one month; nothing else depends on it.
*Views:* 3,000 / **12,000** / 80,000. His name in the title is the lift; his followers rarely convert into YouTube views by themselves. *Orders:* ~10.

### Phase 2: Tools and trust (Feb – May 2027)

**4. Feb 2027: Where did climbing holds come from? (I cast the first one again)**
*Pitch:* Who made the first climbing hold, and who set the first indoor route? What did the first gym in Belgium look like, and who built it? I visit the gyms, meet the route setters who were there (including Pico, who set the first indoor route and still sets today), and trace how rock became plastic. Then I cast a hold the old way. Open loop: the one decision that shaped every wall you've climbed on.
*Why here:* it speaks to every gym climber, and they are the people who buy Sloper King and FingerPrint. It needs interviews and visits, not a long build, and it can be shot in winter. It also sets up the shop launch: "this is why I make my own gear". Each "first" becomes a Short (first hold, first route, first Belgian gym).
*Views:* 3,000 / **20,000** / 150,000. Why: the audience is every climber, the questions are curiosity-gap titles, gyms can show it on their screens, and nobody else has the access. Why not higher: unknown names don't get searched, so the title must sell the question, not the people. *Orders:* ~15 (soft mention, a climbing audience).
*Check first:* archive photo rights, and verify every "first" with two sources before you publish.

**5. Mar 2027: How slopers work (so I built a trainer for them)**
*Pitch:* In the 2025 World Championships three slopers in a row ended my qualification route. Why do slopers feel so different from edges? I'll explain the friction and wrist physics, then show the tool I built, with real climbers in the gym trying it.
*Counterintuitive fact (use as the open loop in the first minute):* **"Did you know it's easier to hang on a sloper if you add mass?"** Test it on camera: hang from the same sloper with and without added weight (cable machine or weight vest) and show the result. Verify the explanation before scripting: with simple Coulomb friction, extra mass should not change the slip limit, so the real reason (friction that rises with contact pressure, and the load pulling the hand into the hold) needs to be backed by your own test, not by a claim.
*Why here:* the product story, now with an audience that trusts you. The batch is already made and has been selling quietly through Instagram and gyms since November.
*Views:* 2,000 / **9,000** / 40,000. *Orders:* ~22 (the product is the topic).

**6. Apr 2027: Why I build my own climbing gear (and how to get it)**
*Pitch:* Who owns climbing equipment, who can change it, and why it barely changed for decades? Then my answer: open files for makers, finished sets for everyone else, and a preorder with a clear funding goal and refund terms. (Same structure as OpenDrone's "Who Owns Your Drone?")
*Why here:* the launch video, after five videos of trust. One clear offer with a deadline.
*Views:* 5,000 / **20,000** / 80,000. *Orders:* ~50, the highest of the series.

**7. May 2027: How to make a see-through climbing wall**
*Pitch:* Every climbing video films a back. I thermoform transparent tiles, bolt them into a wall and film from the other side, so you see the face, the hands and the feet at the same time. Open loop: why the first tile cracked.
*Why here:* reach video. Pure visual novelty with a title anyone understands.
*Views:* 4,000 / **25,000** / 250,000. *Orders:* ~8.

### Phase 3: Flagship and depth (Jun – Sep 2027)

**8. Jun 2027: How a prosthetic foot works (so I built one for climbing)**
*Pitch:* Walking feet store and return energy; climbing feet need to stand on 2 mm edges. I'll show how a prosthetic foot is built, why a climbing foot needs different rules, and my own build, tested on the wall. The intro can use your five years of building a robot hummingbird as the proof that you do this for real.
*Why here:* the flagship. It needs the longest build, so it goes where the channel can carry it. It joins your engineering with your story, with no pity angle.
*Views:* 7,000 / **40,000** / 500,000. Highest ceiling of the series, with press potential in Belgium. *Orders:* ~8.

**9. Jul 2027: Climbing without a foot: the complete guide**
*Pitch:* Technique, balance and the prosthetics debate for leg amputee climbers, and what every climber can learn from it about footwork. With and without a prosthesis, the physics of losing plantar flexion, and how the classification rules work.
*Why here:* a search-friendly video in the core domain with little competition; it also opens the para-climbing community.
*Views:* 1,500 / **7,000** / 30,000. *Orders:* ~3.

**10. Aug 2027: How hard do you actually pull? (I put force sensors in the holds)**
*Pitch:* Load cells in the holds, live on screen. What do you pull on a hold during a dynamic move, and where does the force go? I'll compare an easy and a hard move on the same wall.
*Views:* 2,500 / **12,000** / 60,000. *Orders:* ~10.

**11. Sep 2027: I analysed 50,000 Kilter Board climbs**
*Pitch:* A huge community has logged tens of thousands of climbs. Which holds does everyone ignore, which grades are secretly easy or hard, and what does the data say about how to train? (Check the board's terms of use for data access first.)
*Views:* 4,000 / **20,000** / 120,000. *Orders:* ~10.

### Phase 4: Insider and science (Oct – Dec 2027)

**12. Oct 2027: I wore eye-tracking glasses at a World Cup route preview**
*Pitch:* Climbers get a few minutes to study a route before climbing it. Where do the best look, and what do they see that the rest of us miss? (Needs IFSC permission and the glasses.)
*Views:* 4,000 / **20,000** / 100,000. *Orders:* ~10.

**13. Nov 2027: How "the pump" works (I measured my forearm)**
*Pitch:* A muscle oxygen sensor on the forearm and a finger-prick lactate test between attempts. What actually happens when your arms swell and stop working, and why can some people climb through it? Natural tie-in to the Sloper King as a pump trainer.
*Views:* 3,000 / **15,000** / 80,000. *Orders:* ~12.

**14. Dec 2027: How rubber grips (a friction test with Hannes Van Duysen)**
*Pitch:* Why do climbing shoes stick to a wall that looks smooth? We test rubbers on different surfaces and temperatures with a world-class climber, and explain the physics that nobody gets right.
*Views:* 3,000 / **15,000** / 70,000. *Orders:* ~8. *Timing:* a second Christmas, with a new product drop if one is ready.

### Phase 5: Virals and the road to LA (Jan – Jun 2028)

**15. Jan 2028: I built 3 extreme prosthetics**
*Pitch:* A pneumatic jump booster, an extra-long leg, a tool mount. They're ridiculous, and each teaches something about leverage and energy.
*Views:* 8,000 / **60,000** / 800,000. Variance is huge. *Orders:* ~12.

**16. Feb 2028: Climbing chalk under a microscope**
*Pitch:* 500× on skin and on rock. What does chalk actually do, and is liquid chalk better than block?
*Views:* 4,000 / **25,000** / 200,000. Low effort, and it makes good Shorts. *Orders:* ~10.

**17. Mar 2028: I built an AI that tracks my centre of mass**
*Pitch:* Computer vision on a climbing video, with a live graph of balance and hip movement. Where do I waste energy, and can software see it before I do?
*Views:* 2,500 / **15,000** / 80,000. *Orders:* ~5.

**18. Apr 2028: How I train as a Paralympic climber**
*Pitch:* A real week from morning to night, around a job, with the numbers on screen. What the best actually do, and what I would change.
*Views:* 4,000 / **20,000** / 80,000. *Orders:* ~16.

**19. May 2028: The qualification for LA 2028**
*Pitch:* The race to qualify, from the inside: the rules, the pressure, and what I changed because of what the data showed.
*Views:* 8,000 / **40,000** / 200,000. *Orders:* ~20.

**20. Jun 2028: Two months to LA**
*Pitch:* The final stretch: the last build, the last test, the last training block. Everything the series has set up pays off here.
*Views:* 8,000 / **40,000** / 300,000. *Orders:* ~20.

*After this:* July–August 2028 is Shorts and Instagram from LA, and a **Games film** as video 21. Its range is wide (about 8k–300k) and depends on the result.

## Totals
- **Long-form views (90 days, summed):** Low ≈ **80k**, **Likely ≈ 435k**. A single breakout of 250k–800k (#7, #8 or #15) is what changes the channel; the sum of the "High" column isn't a forecast.
- **Subscribers:** if 1.5% of views become subscribers (OpenDrone's ratio is roughly that), Likely is about **8–12k subscribers** by the Games, with tail views included.
- **Orders from the videos themselves:** about **270**, roughly **€12k** at an average of ~€45. The long tail, Instagram, gyms and the newsletter add to this.

### Three scenarios (decided by the first three videos)
| Scenario | Long-form views, all 20 videos | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | ~145k | Videos 1–3 average under 2,000 views: divide later estimates by 3 |
| **Likely** | **~435k** | The format works partly |
| OpenDrone-like | 1.5M+ | The format clicks: videos from #4 onwards perform about 4× the Likely numbers |

OpenDrone's 834k video is the best of their 38 videos, and it came after years of small ones, as part of a series, with a large collaborator. Treat 834k as a ceiling you can reach for, not as the typical result. The aim of 20 videos is to buy many chances at a breakout, and I'd guess (not calculate) roughly a one-in-three chance that at least one passes 250k.

## Why this order
1. **Prove the format first (1–3), using what already exists.** A tool that is built, a lab test that needs only your workshop, and a well-known name. These tell you quickly whether the format works for you.
2. **Earn the right to sell (4–5), then sell (6).** The history of holds brings in every climber, the sloper story makes the product concrete, and #6 gives the one clear offer, with a deadline.
3. **Spend the big builds where the channel can use them (7–8).** The wall and the bionic foot need an audience that can carry a breakout. Placing them after the launch means a hit sends people to a channel that already has a shop.
4. **Alternate effort.** Heavy builds (2, 7, 8, 15) are followed by lighter data or insider videos (9–14, 16–18), so training weeks survive.
5. **Let the story take over near LA (18–20).** By then the audience knows the person, and the deadline carries the last videos.

## What this changes in practice
- **Shop:** Sloper King and FingerPrint sell quietly from now on (Instagram, newsletter, gyms). You don't need to push them on YouTube until #6.
- **Honest limit:** this series builds trust and reach more than sales. If you want to sell a lot, add products that makers and climbers both buy (kits, test-rig plans, the next FingerPrint versions), and run a launch like #6 for each one.
- **Risks to check early:** Jakob's schedule (#3), archive photo rights and fact-checking (#4), IFSC permission for eye tracking (#12), data terms for the Kilter board (#11), and product liability for load-bearing 3D prints (before #2 and #6).
